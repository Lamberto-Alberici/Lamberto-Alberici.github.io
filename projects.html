NCAA Men's Final Four Analytics Challenge — 3rd Place

Python Tableau XGBoost CatBoost SHAP Machine Learning Sports Analytics
View Full Report View Interactive Dashboards Official Competition Page

Collaborated with Owen Nguyen, Tran Le, Ahnaf Labib, and Dita Tran

Overview

Advanced to the finals of the 2026 NCAA Men's Final Four Analytics Challenge, placing 3rd nationally out of hundreds of competing university teams. Built a two-stage predictive system to classify tournament selection and predict tournament seeding for all 350+ Division I programs, presenting findings to a panel of NCAA executives and business judges in Indianapolis on April 6th, 2026.

Approach

  • Augmented the official NCAA dataset with 16 additional features from KenPom (advanced efficiency metrics) and ESPN (traditional box score statistics), engineering 100+ total features including quadrant records, efficiency margins, résumé scores, Pseudo-Elo ratings, and interaction terms
  • Built a binary XGBoost classifier with class weighting to handle a highly imbalanced dataset (~18% positive rate), predicting tournament qualification; validated using GroupKFold cross-validation by season to prevent data leakage across years
  • Developed a CatBoost regression model predicting overall tournament seed (1–68) among qualified teams, using SHAP analysis to identify the top 25 most predictive features
  • Conducted correlation-based feature screening keeping features with absolute correlation above 0.30 with the target, then reduced to top 25 features via SHAP importance
  • Built interactive Tableau dashboards for the final presentation, including a dynamic parameter-driven scatter plot and box plot that updated simultaneously across all features, and a dual-axis chart revealing a counterintuitive U-shaped relationship in non-conference scheduling

Key Results

  • Classification model achieved a test ROC-AUC of 98.01% with only a 1.90% overfitting gap and an F1 score of 83.6% — meaning if we randomly pick one tournament team and one non-tournament team, the model ranks the tournament team higher 98% of the time
  • Seeding regression model achieved a mean RMSE of 5.33 seed positions under GroupKFold validation with a modest overfitting gap of 2.48
  • SHAP analysis confirmed that NET-based metrics dominate predictions, validating that the NCAA's own evaluation tool is the strongest signal — the committee's process is internally consistent and learnable
  • Q1 losses don't penalize tournament chances, but accumulating them by seeking elite competition actually earns better seeds — a counterintuitive, data-backed finding
  • Non-conference scheduling follows a U-shaped pattern: elite teams with under 30% non-conference win rates make the tournament at the highest rate (44.9%) by voluntarily scheduling the toughest opponents in the country

Business Impact

  • Coaches and Athletic Directors: Quantifies how individual scheduling decisions influence NET rank, SOS, and Quadrant 1 record — enabling programs to identify bubble risk 3–5 weeks before Selection Sunday while they still have agency to adjust
  • Conference Offices: Provides a unified forward-looking view of how every program's metrics evolve in real time, enabling coordinated scheduling strategies to maximize at-large bids and conference distributions
  • Media and Analysts: Generates consistent, auditable predictions that separate at-large selection from seeding, offering a defensible data-driven foundation that eliminates the inconsistencies of subjective bracketology
  • The NCAA: Demonstrates that while selection is officially subjective, it follows consistent learnable patterns — enabling the NCAA to use this system as a consistency audit to flag anomalous selections before finalization

Motherhood Wage Penalty Analysis

Stata Regression Analysis Statistical Modeling
View Full Paper

Overview

Investigated whether mothers in the U.S. face a wage penalty using regression analysis on microdata from the 2024 IPUMS CPS ASEC survey. Analyzed the relationship between number of children and wages while controlling for education, experience, hours worked, marital status, race, ethnicity, and occupation. Applied survey weights to account for complex sampling methods and ensure statistical accuracy.

Approach

  • Built multiple OLS regression models with log-transformed income as the dependent variable
  • Controlled for key variables: education years, weekly hours, work experience, marital status, race/ethnicity, and occupation
  • Applied ASEC survey weights to correct for sampling bias and improve model accuracy
  • Calculated robust standard errors and 95% confidence intervals to assess statistical significance
  • Analyzed wage effects across different income groups ($20K-$60K vs. >$60K) to examine how the penalty varies by earnings level

Key Findings

  • Initial models suggested a negative correlation between number of children and wages, but the effect lost statistical significance when proper survey weights were applied
  • The weighted model showed a 0.25% decrease in wages per child, but confidence intervals included zero, indicating no conclusive evidence of a motherhood wage penalty in 2024
  • Lower-income mothers ($20K-$60K) experienced a 0.76% wage decrease per child, while higher-income mothers (>$60K) saw a 1.13% wage increase, suggesting the penalty disproportionately affects lower-wage workers
  • Results contrast with previous studies from 2004-2013 that found significant penalties, potentially indicating improved workplace equity or methodological differences
  • Limitations: The analysis acknowledges potential omitted variable bias (e.g., motivation, career choices) and sampling selection bias. While this study found no statistically significant penalty, mothers may still face wage disadvantages that require different methodological approaches or state-level analysis to detect

Route to Victory: NFL Passing Analytics Using Player-Tracking Data

Python Tableau Machine Learning Regression Analysis
View Full Paper

Collaborated with Connor Agler and Maksymilian Hildt

Overview

Comprehensive analysis of offensive passing performance in the 2023 NFL season using player-tracking data from the 2026 NFL Big Data Bowl. Conducted three interconnected analyses, pre-snap prediction, route effectiveness, and ball-tracking efficiency, to provide a 360-degree view of the passing game across 396,796 targeted receiver observations.

Approach

  • Pre-snap Analysis: Built OLS regression model predicting yards gained using formation, alignment, coverage indicators, and situational variables
  • Route Effectiveness: Developed six models (4 OLS, 2 logistic) analyzing 12 route types using yards gained and pass completion as dependent variables, with varying control variables
  • Ball Tracking Efficiency (BTE): Created custom metric (BTE = Ideal Distance / Actual Distance) to measure route geometry; applied OLS, Ridge, and Lasso models to identify predictors
  • Built interactive Tableau dashboard allowing filtering by route, coverage type, down, territory, quarter, and pass result

Key Findings

  • Pre-snap model achieved R² of 0.531, with pass completion status as the strongest predictor; play action and QB scrambles each increased expected yards by approximately 1 yard
  • Route effectiveness revealed Post, Corner, Go, and Wheel routes maximized yards gained (6-8 yards above baseline), while Flat, Hitch, Out, and Cross routes achieved highest completion rates
  • Cross route emerged as the most balanced option overall, ranking top 5 in both yards gained and completion probability
  • BTE analysis showed receivers run paths 23% longer than direct distance on average (BTE = 0.81), reflecting strategic route complexity rather than inefficiency
  • Player position, team offensive schemes, and route type were strongest BTE predictors; complex scheme teams (HOU, SF) had 7-9% lower BTE, while vertical passing teams (ATL, LV) had 2.6% higher BTE

European Football Performance Analysis: Statistical Predictors of Success

Tableau Data Visualization Regression Analysis Sports Analytics
View Interactive Dashboards

Overview

Multi-level statistical analysis of European football examining 12,178 players and 34,540 games across 13 years (2012-2025). Combined OLS and logistic regression with interactive Tableau dashboards to identify key predictors of individual goal-scoring, financial success, and match outcomes across Europe's top 5 leagues.

Approach

  • Aggregated and cleaned 13 years of performance and financial data from Transfermarkt across 5 European leagues (Premier League, La Liga, Serie A, Bundesliga, Ligue 1)
  • Built OLS regression model analyzing 6,420 players to identify statistical predictors of goal-scoring ability (R²=0.532)
  • Developed logistic regression model on 34,540 games predicting match outcomes from team characteristics (Pseudo R²=0.574)
  • Analyzed financial data for Europe's 20 richest clubs examining revenue sources and wage sustainability against UEFA guidelines
  • Created 5 interactive Tableau dashboards visualizing regression results, performance trends, and financial patterns with dynamic filtering

Key Findings

  • Assists per game is the strongest predictor of individual goal-scoring (β=0.319***), with 160x more impact than height
  • Financial advantage exists but varies - wage efficiency ranges from 55% (sustainable) to 110% (crisis) of revenue across top clubs
  • Goals scored dominates match outcomes (932% increase in win odds per goal), far outweighing all other factors
  • Home advantage doubles win probability (+109%), while opponent position creates 3x variation in difficulty
